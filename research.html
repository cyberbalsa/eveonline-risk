Warfront is a single-player, RISK-inspired fan game. Its map comes from public EVE data, its computer commanders run locally, and every campaign uses the same rules for you and the bots.

The Caldari–Gallente map

The public ESI faction-war roster was retrieved on September 22, 2026. Filtering the original owner to Caldari State (500001) or Gallente Federation (500004) produces 90 systems. Filtering current occupation would confuse warzone membership with shifting ownership.

System and stargate endpoints supply 110 direct connections, forming one connected graph. All 90 systems and all 110 links appear on the board. Routes through systems outside the warzone are excluded. Star positions come directly from CCP’s position2D in Static Data Export build 3528119, the coordinates used by EVE’s in-game 2D map. One uniform scale and a screen-Y reflection preserve its orientation, relative positions, and aspect ratio; no stars are rearranged. The board’s rings, labels, and overlays are original rendering code. Campaign occupancy is a game scenario, not live EVE control. CCP’s map-data guide explains the distinction between in-game 2D coordinates and the physical 3D coordinates. The extracted 90-system position data and provenance are included.

The 15 real constellations are this board's territory groups. Each bonus is a game adaptation: rounded (system count + number of border systems) / 3, with a minimum of one. These payouts are not EVE's faction warfare rewards. EVE's actual conflict mechanics are documented by CCP's Factional Warfare guide.

Rules and adaptations

The baseline is Hasbro's classic RISK instructions: territory income, groups, maximum three attack dice and two defense dice, defender-wins-ties combat, garrisons, a conquest card, progressive set exchange, elimination card transfer, and one connected fortification. The in-game field manual explains the playable rules.

Each commander receives 55 starting fleets. Classic conquest deals systems randomly. Militia campaigns establish territorial home areas and vary the border zone. It uses 15 constellation bonuses and a 92-card deck: 90 system cards, evenly divided among three hull classes, plus two wilds. Defenders automatically use their maximum dice. Militia war adds two teams of two, while total conquest is a four-commander free-for-all. Allies share victory, but not fleets, card hands, fortification routes, or constellation bonuses. Captured-system movement is chosen after the final roll. Blitz commits maximum dice until capture or one fleet remains; you can stop between volleys.

Militia frontlines and capture rules

Each commander has a fixed, connected six-system home area anchored at Hykanima and Ishomilken for Caldari, and Eugales and Fliet for Gallente. At least two frontline systems begin under each militia’s direct control. Additional original-faction territory is held, while systems near the original faction boundary vary by seed. Expansion into that middle requires adjacency to a held militia system; a mix of neutral garrisons remains. These starting guarantees are board design choices, not minimum counts imposed by EVE.

Neutral garrisons belong to no commander and take no turns, collect no income, and cannot claim sovereignty. Each neutral-held system retains a Caldari or Gallente occupancy claim. Clearing a friendly-claim garrison simply establishes commander control. Enemy-claim hubs require the full capture sequence.

CCP’s FW guide defines Frontline, Command Operations, and Rearguard by neighboring militia occupancy. CCP’s FW patch notes document defensive plex rewards, vulnerable systems, VP thresholds, and occupancy updates at downtime. This game follows the sequence: offensive plexes increase contested progress; defensive plexes reduce it; the enemy hub must reach 100% vulnerability before assault; a defeated hub is lost until occupancy updates.

For the board game, 4–8 plex operations per commander turn replace real-time site timers. Frontline / Command Operations / Rearguard operations change contested progress by 25 / 15 / 5 points. These are game-tuned values, not EVE’s live VP payouts. A fleet of at least two launches an operation locally or through one direct gate. Lost systems lock until the next round boundary, our downtime equivalent; they provide no income and cannot be used to advance. Occupancy resets to uncontested after the flip. LP, advantage objectives, and hull-restricted site classes are outside this adaptation. CCP’s later VP and site-spawn changes illustrate why EVE’s real values should not be confused with these turn-based ones.

Previously saved campaigns keep their classic capture rules and fleet allocations. New FW setup and capture rules apply when deploying a militia campaign. Changing map coordinates does not change system IDs or invalidate existing saves.

Researching the bots

Aidan O’Connor and Alexander Lawless's RISK bot project explores heuristic policies and learned parameters for reinforcement, attacks, and fortification. It supports using an interpretable heuristic as a practical starting point. Warfront's implementation is original; no source code or trained weights were copied.

Reinforcement Learning in the Game of RISK studies state evaluation, predicted win chances, attack thresholds, and learned policies in a simplified environment. Our design borrows the idea of using combat odds to gate attacks. Its reported performance and thresholds do not transfer directly to this 90-system board.

Warfront enumerates all possible dice outcomes, then uses dynamic programming to calculate full-battle capture probabilities for armies up to 500 fleets. Larger armies use a bounded logistic approximation, marked ≈ in the interface. Planning favors constellation completion, breaking enemy income, eliminating a commander, earning the turn's card, and moving unused fleets toward threatened borders.

Recruit lowers these floors by 12 percentage points. Elite raises them by seven. These are risk profiles, not a proven ranking of playing strength. Every bot gets the same dice and income rules. The planner receives a projection that excludes the random generator's state, the deck order, and other players' card identities. Fictional commander names imply no real pilot endorsement or behavior. These bots play this browser game only.

In 96 seeded classic conquest games, one heuristic commander won 87 games (90.6%) against three fixed policies; nine remained unfinished at the 600-turn horizon. All four seats and three styles rotated. The baseline stacks a strong border, attacks at a 1.5× fleet advantage, trades available sets, and moves rear reserves forward. This is a narrow classic-rules comparison, not a benchmark of the new FW capture mode or evidence of strength against expert humans. The full benchmark records results, seeds, and limits. Run npm run benchmark -- 96 820000 to reproduce.

The old colored icons

The interface uses unmodified icons from CCP's Phoebe 1.0 icon pack (2014), reused from the companion EVE Monopoly project. The icon manifest preserves their original paths and SHA-256 checksums.

Caracal, Drake, Catalyst, and Dominix portraits are local copies of CCP's EVE Image Server renders, also reused from that project. The SVG map, interface layout, and Warfront wordmark are project-authored.

Client sounds

The requested media mount was empty in the development environment. This version reuses the companion project's prepared local sounds, retaining its complete source and processing manifest. CCP UI sounds come from Mad_Guns22's EVE audio recordings. Cargo, notification, and skill-completion cues accompany commands, captures, and turn changes. The bundled capacitor and structure warning clips are retained for future cues.

The dice roll and fleet-transfer thruster are supplemental recordings from Kenney's Casino Audio and Sci-fi Sounds packs, under CC0. They are not EVE client audio. Sound effects start after interaction, have independent mute and volume controls, and stop in hidden tabs. Audio failure never blocks a rules action.

Project and verification

Like the companion project, Warfront is static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with relative asset URLs and GitHub Pages hosting. It needs no backend, login, API key, runtime AI service, or live EVE API. Google Fonts are optional, with system fallbacks. Saves live in this browser; Export and Import transfer campaigns between devices. Browser data clearing can remove saves, so export a campaign you want to keep. An import retains the previous campaign under a separate recovery key when browser storage allows.

Rules tests cover geography, dice probabilities, ownership, card accounting, eliminations, saved occupations, fortification, team victory, asset hashes, 96 classic campaigns, twelve complete FW campaigns, and 96 starting-layout seeds. FW checks verify neutral accounting, minimum frontlines, plexing, vulnerability, pending flips, downtime, and save migration. Browser tests cover enlistment, combat, cards, reloads, bot pauses, small viewports, keyboard selection, audio decoding, import/export, project-subpath hosting, neutral system inspection, and the plex-to-hub capture flow.
