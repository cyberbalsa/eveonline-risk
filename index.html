 CAPSULEER FIELD MANUAL

Plan. Commit. Conquer.

01 / REINFORCE

Build your fleet.

Each turn grants one fleet per three systems you personally own, rounded down, with a minimum of three. Hold a whole constellation for its listed bonus. Select a friendly system and deploy all your reserves before attacking.

02 / ATTACK

Take the next gate.

Select your system, then an enemy connected by a stargate. In FW campaigns, complete offensive plexes to make an enemy hub vulnerable first. Then roll up to three attack dice against up to two defense dice. Higher dice win; ties favor the defender. Always leave one fleet behind. You can stop attacking at any time.

03 / FORTIFY

Secure the frontier.

Make one optional transfer through a continuous chain of your own systems. Keep one fleet at the origin. Then end your turn. Conquering at least one system earns exactly one card when you finish attacking.

04 / TERRITORY CARDS

Call in reserves.

Trade three matching hulls, one of each, or a valid wild set during reinforcement. Sets pay 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, then five more each time, shared across all commanders. Five cards force a trade. An owned system in a traded set gets two extra fleets.

Know your objective.

Militia war: you and the orange commander share victory. Eliminate both enemy commanders. Allies cannot attack each other; fleets and constellation income stay under each commander's control. If you are eliminated, you can spectate your ally.

Total conquest: capture all 90 systems. Militia identity is cosmetic and every other commander is an enemy. Elimination transfers the defeated commander's cards; six or more cards trigger immediate exchanges until at most four remain. Deploy those fleets before continuing the attack.

Know the map.

The map uses CCP’s actual in-game 2D positions for all 90 warzone systems, with all 110 direct ESI stargate links. North, relative positions, and proportions match that layout. Militia-colored rings show occupancy; fleet numbers use commander colors. Gray garrisons are neutral, and amber dashed gates mark frontlines. Labels yield to nearby labels and reappear as you zoom or hover. Select a constellation, search, drag, pinch, or use zoom controls to explore.

FW occupancy and neutral systems.

Militia campaigns begin with six connected home systems per commander, at least two held frontlines per militia, and additional original-faction territory. The border zone varies between militia control and neutral garrisons. Neutral means no commander controls the fleet; every system still has a Caldari or Gallente occupancy claim. Starting strongholds can be conquered normally.

Plex → vulnerable hub → lost → occupancy flip. Each commander receives 4–8 plex operations per turn: four plus one per ten ready systems, capped at eight. Launch from a system you control with at least two fleets, into that system or a directly connected one. Offensive plexing raises enemy contested progress; defensive plexing lowers progress in friendly militia space. At 100% the enemy infrastructure hub becomes vulnerable to dice combat. Clearing a neutral garrison in your own militia’s space does not change its sovereignty and needs no offensive plexing.

Defeating a vulnerable hub marks the system lost. Occupy it, then wait for the next round’s downtime before its militia claim flips and contested progress resets. Lost systems cannot be attacked, plexed, reinforced, or used for fortification, and provide no income until the flip. A militia victory requires all systems secured and all pending flips resolved.

Frontline systems border enemy militia occupancy. Command Operations systems neighbor a friendly frontline; others are Rearguard. Neutral garrison ownership does not alter this classification. This turn-based adaptation uses 25 / 15 / 5 contested points per operation in those three states; these rates, operation budgets, and round-based downtime replace EVE’s real timers and VP quantities. LP, advantage objectives, and ship-restricted plex classes are not simulated. Classic conquest and older saved campaigns retain direct RISK captures.

A fair fight.

Bots use visible territory, fleet counts, and their own cards. They cannot inspect future dice or the deck. Aggressive, defensive, and balanced commanders evaluate combat odds, constellation control, and border pressure. Difficulty changes their appetite for risk; it grants no extra fleets. The difficulty labels are not a measured strength ranking.

Board adaptations: 55 starting fleets per commander; randomized ownership in classic conquest; anchored militia territory and a variable neutral middle in FW; 15 constellation bonuses; 90 system cards and two wilds; defender always rolls the maximum available dice. Blitz uses maximum attack dice until capture or only one fleet remains. Capture odds assume both sides keep rolling maximum dice. At armies above 500, odds are an approximation.
